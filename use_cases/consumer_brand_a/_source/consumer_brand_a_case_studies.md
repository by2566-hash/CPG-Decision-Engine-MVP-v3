# **Consumer Brand A - UGC Creator Program**

**1. CAC and CPMs declined following the introduction of a structured
UGC creator program on Meta**

**Type:** Acquisition Efficiency, secondary: Creative

**Problem signal:** Meta acquisition costs rising with no change in
spend, audience, or campaign structure, isolating creative composition
as the primary variable.

**Details we have:**

-   Before UGC program: Meta CAC at \$210 and CPMs at \$25

-   After UGC program launched: Blended daily Meta CAC reduced to \$149
    > and CPMs to \$11

-   Before UGC program: 100% of spend allocated to brand-produced
    > creative

-   1,000 creator applications received in the first week with 25
    > creators selected at \$250--\$500 per piece (\$10K total
    > investment)

-   UGC creative: \$58K weekly spend at \$141 CAC

-   Non-UGC creative: \$200 CAC during the same period

**Trigger logic:**

-   If Meta CAC rises with no meaningful change in spend, audience, or
    > campaign structure, audit creative composition before adjusting
    > media

-   If CPMs are elevated and not improving, audit creative composition
    > as one variable before adjusting bids or audiences

-   If the active creative pool is heavily skewed to one format,
    > diversify creative mix before drawing conclusions about channel or
    > audience performance

**Checks / analysis path:**

-   Audit active creative by content type and confirm format
    > distribution

-   Identify whether performance is concentrated in a single format

**Action:**

-   Test underrepresented creative formats when performance is
    > concentrated

-   Scale spend behind top-performing creative types

**Observed result / use-case value:** Introducing UGC reduced Meta CAC
from \$210 to \$149 and CPMs from \$25 to \$11, consistent with creative
composition as the primary driver of performance

# **Consumer Brand A - Compliance UX and CVR Collapse**

**2. Legally required health data consent pop-up caused a sudden CVR
collapse across all traffic sources**

**Type:** Conversion / Merchandising, secondary: Compliance UX

**Problem signal:** Site CVR dropped suddenly and materially with no
change in traffic quality, spend, or creative.

**Details we have:**

-   CVR dropped from 2.5% to under 1% overnight

-   Primary conversion flow was a personalized quiz collecting health
    > data before delivering a product recommendation

-   Legal required a health data consent pop-up before quiz entry with
    > language that was clinical and implemented without UX testing

-   Paid traffic was routed into the same quiz flow via UTMs

**Trigger logic:**

-   If CVR drops suddenly across paid and organic, audit the on-site
    > experience before adjusting campaigns or media

-   If the drop coincides with a recent site or flow change, audit that
    > change before questioning traffic quality or media performance

-   If legal or compliance changes have recently been deployed, treat UX
    > friction as the first hypothesis before adjusting spend or
    > targeting

**Checks / analysis path:**

-   Map CVR trend against timeline of recent site and flow changes

-   Simulate the full conversion path as a new visitor using an
    > incognito window

-   Identify all friction points introduced recently, such as consent
    > pop-ups, legal copy, flow changes

-   Confirm whether paid and organic share the same conversion path

**Action:**

-   Remove or redesign friction introduced by compliance changes and
    > provide alternative paths for users who exit the primary flow

**Observed result / use-case value:** CVR recovered 3x following the
redesign. Fix applied to core flow benefited both paid and organic
simultaneously. CAC improved as conversion efficiency was restored.

# **Consumer Brand B - Offer-Heavy Creative**

**3. Offer-led retargeting creative reduced CAC by converting
lower-quality customers**

**Type:** Acquisition Efficiency, secondary: Retention diagnostic

**Problem signal:** Retargeting CAC improved materially, driven by
lower-quality conversions that weakened repeat purchase behavior.

**Details we have:**

-   CAC running at \~\$155 across warm audiences

-   Existing creative did not clearly lead with an offer proposition

-   New static creative introduced with bold first-time offer and
    > sustainability messaging

-   CAC improved from \~\$155 to \~\$76

-   Within 60--75 days, second order rate declined materially for
    > Meta-driven cohorts

-   Early third order rate declined alongside second order rate

-   Creative was replaced with value-led messaging

**Trigger logic:**

-   If CAC improves on a retargeting audience, do not scale until repeat
    > purchase behavior is validated

-   If creative introduces offers or urgency, treat cohort quality as at
    > risk and evaluate repeat purchase behavior

**Checks / analysis path:**

-   Compare CAC and second order before and after creative change

-   Track second order rate by creative cohort at 60 and 90 days

-   Evaluate early third order rate alongside second order rate

-   Break out creative mix (offer-led versus value-led) to isolate
    > driver of efficiency

-   Compare 30/60/90-day revenue per user across cohorts

**Action:**

-   Do not scale retargeting efficiency gains without retention
    > validation

-   Pull or rebalance creative immediately if cohort quality declines

**Observed result / use-case value:** Retargeting CAC improved 51% from
\$155 to \$76, but the gain was driven by lower-quality conversions from
offer-led creative. Within 60--75 days, second order rate for
Meta-driven cohorts declined materially, confirming downstream LTV
degradation. Creative was pulled to prevent scaling lower-value cohorts.
Established rule: CAC improvements in retargeting are only valid if
repeat purchase behavior holds.
